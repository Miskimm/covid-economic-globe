# Problem Statement v2 (DECO7180: COVID-19 x Global Economy)

## Context
Public COVID-19 and macroeconomic datasets are widely available, but non-expert users often struggle to interpret cross-country, cross-time relationships between health shocks and economic outcomes. Existing tools either prioritize raw data access (high cognitive load) or simplified headlines (low analytical depth), leaving a gap in interpretable, evidence-grounded understanding.

## Primary Users

### User Group A (Primary)
- **Who**: Undergraduate/postgraduate students in policy, economics-adjacent, or design courses.
- **Task Context**: Need to discuss and justify claims about pandemic-economic impact in assignments/presentations.
- **Current Pain**:
  - Can read isolated charts but struggle to connect health and economic metrics over time.
  - Confuse preference for visuals with actual understanding.
  - Difficulty distinguishing correlation patterns from causal claims.

### User Group B (Secondary)
- **Who**: General informed public following policy/economic news.
- **Task Context**: Wants to compare countries and periods without expert tooling.
- **Current Pain**:
  - Overwhelmed by multi-source dashboards.
  - Low trust when data source states/method assumptions are unclear.

## Core Problem
The key problem is **not** that data lacks visualization.  
The key problem is that target users lack a reliable way to **interpret and justify** health-economy relationships across time and countries, while understanding uncertainty, data boundaries, and source credibility.

## Why This Is a Necessary Design Opportunity
Without an interpretation-focused tool:
- users produce shallow or incorrect claims from partial readings;
- cross-country and time-based comparison becomes error-prone;
- confidence and trust judgments are made without transparent evidence.

Therefore, a design intervention is justified if it helps users:
1) interpret linked indicators correctly,
2) make traceable comparisons,
3) separate evidence from assumption,
4) understand confidence limits.

## Scope Boundary
- **In scope**: interpretation support, comparative reasoning, data transparency, trust cues.
- **Out of scope**: epidemiological forecasting, policy recommendation automation, causal inference claims.

## Success Criteria (Pre-Prototype)
1. Users can correctly explain key metrics and their relationship in plain language.
2. Users can compare at least two countries/times with traceable evidence.
3. Users can identify source state and uncertainty limits.
4. Users' "liked interface" and "actually understood content" can be measured separately.
